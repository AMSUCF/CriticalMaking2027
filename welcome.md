## Course Overview

This course uses a newer form of humanities research called critical making. You can think of it as a cross between "making" as in the "maker spaces" that became really popular a few years ago, and "critical" as in "critique" like you'd find in the arts. It's also referred to as research-creation. Whatever you want to call it, the focus is on the process--what you are learning and discovering as you make--rather than the final product. You are making things that are more like prototypes or proofs-of-concept than final products or masterpieces. Embrace the rough edges!

However, that doesn't mean you won't need to spend time and effort on the assignments in this course! Schedule time to engage each week with the course. The assignments are organized so that you continue to build new skills and ways of thinking each week.

The making emphasis of this course works best if you set aside regular time each week to read, make, and reflect. This online course is not "self-paced." There are readings and assignments due each week.

I suggest setting aside time at least three days per week for coursework:

- Time one day per week to carefully read and take notes (Monday?)
- Time a second day per week to complete the critical making assignment (Wednesday?)
- Time a third day per week to post your assignment and your reflection (Friday or Saturday?)
- **Deadlines are Sundays at 11:59 PM.** Each week's discussion stays open for no-penalty late submissions for one week after the deadline.
